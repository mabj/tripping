# Porto

## General Tips

- From the airport to the city, Uber is the easiest option (~€12 to Vila Nova de Gaia). Alternatively, take the Metro purple line (line E) from "Airport" to "Trindade" (€2,30) and then a bus (e.g. Bus 905 towards Gaia, €1,40). More info [here](https://porto.travel/porto-airport/);
- Vila Nova de Gaia, on the other side of the Douro river, is where the Port wine cellars are and it is a good base to stay;
- Porto is hilly and full of cobblestones, so bring comfortable shoes for walking;
- Port wine cellar tours are limited to a certain number of participants per session, so book in advance;

## Things to Do

- Do a Port wine tasting in one of the historic cellars in Gaia (e.g. Ferreira, active since 1751);
- Do the "PORTO VINTAGE VISIT" tour at [Caves Ferreira](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Caves%20Ferreira%2C%20Vila%20Nova%20de%20Gaia) in Gaia (€55, 1h30) - highly recommended: a guided visit through the historic cellars and the Ferreira Vintage Museum, ending with a tasting of four Ferreira Vintages in the Sala dos Azulejos;
- Cross the historic [Ponte Luís I](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Ponte%20Lu%C3%ADs%20I%2C%20Porto) bridge on foot between Porto and Vila Nova de Gaia (the upper deck has the best views of the city);
- Visit the [Porto Cathedral (Sé do Porto)](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=S%C3%A9%20do%20Porto%2C%20Porto) and enjoy the view of the city from its terrace;
- Take a day trip to Matosinhos, by the sea and easy to reach by Metro from Porto (blue line / line A to the "Matosinhos Sul" stop). Walk along the beach, check out the Anémona monument and visit the [Mercado Municipal de Matosinhos](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Mercado%20Municipal%20de%20Matosinhos%2C%20Matosinhos);
- Walk a city tour around Porto's old town;
- Go for street food and a pub crawl in the evening;
- Have lunch at Mercado Beira-Rio, with lots of local and international options;
- Rent a bike in Vila Nova de Gaia and ride the coastal route south from Gaia to Valadares and Miramar (a 10-20 km trip, depending on how far you go and whether you ride back). There is good infrastructure along the way (dedicated cycle path and boardwalk sections by the sea) and the route passes a lot of beautiful beaches, such as [Praia de Lavadores](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Praia%20de%20Lavadores%2C%20Vila%20Nova%20de%20Gaia), [Praia dos Salgueiros](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Praia%20dos%20Salgueiros%2C%20Vila%20Nova%20de%20Gaia) and [Praia da Sereia da Costa Verde](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Praia%20da%20Sereia%20da%20Costa%20Verde%2C%20Valadares). The ride ends at the [Capela do Senhor da Pedra](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Capela%20do%20Senhor%20da%20Pedra%2C%20Miramar), a small chapel built on the rocks right on the beach, a great spot to stop before heading back;
- Take a boat trip along the Douro river up to the Douro Valley and its wineries (quintas), with wine tastings and views of the terraced vineyards. Keep in mind that this is a full-day trip, around 12 hours on average, and that lunch is usually included;

## Museums

- Ferreira Vintage Museum (inside Caves Ferreira) - Part of the "Porto Vintage Visit" tour, which ends with a tasting of four Ferreira Vintages in the Sala dos Azulejos, decorated with traditional Portuguese blue tiles;

## Food to Try

- Francesinha - Porto's signature sandwich: bread, cured meats and steak covered in melted cheese and a hot beer-and-tomato sauce, usually served with fries;
- Tripas à moda do Porto (tripe stew with white beans) - the most traditional dish of the city, and the reason why people from Porto are nicknamed "tripeiros";
- "Cachorrinho" - the Portuguese variation of a hot dog: sausage in thin crispy bread, pressed and grilled, cut into small pieces and served with a spicy sauce;

### Where to Eat Francesinha

- [Café Santiago](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Caf%C3%A9%20Santiago%2C%20Porto) (Rua de Passos Manuel, city centre) - The classic, old-school choice, often with a queue. Best with a cold Super Bock;
- [Cervejaria Brasão](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Cervejaria%20Bras%C3%A3o%2C%20Porto) (several locations, e.g. Coliseu and Aliados) - More modern and trendy, nicely decorated and with very good service;
- [Capa Negra II](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Capa%20Negra%20II%2C%20Porto) (Rua do Campo Alegre) - A long-standing favourite among locals;
- [Francesinha Café](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Francesinha%20Caf%C3%A9%2C%20Porto) (Rua da Alegria) - A bit away from the tourist area, consistently rated as one of the best in the city;
- [O Afonso](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=O%20Afonso%20francesinha%2C%20Porto) (Rua da Torrinha) - Small and traditional, made famous by an Anthony Bourdain visit;

### Where to Eat Cachorrinho

- [Gazela Cachorrinhos da Batalha](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Gazela%20Cachorrinhos%20da%20Batalha%2C%20Porto) (Travessa do Cimo de Vila 4, near Praça da Batalha) - Open since 1962 and widely considered the birthplace of the cachorrinho. Small, always busy, cash preferred and lunch is the best time to go. There is a second house a couple of minutes away if the counter is full;
